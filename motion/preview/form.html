Concept: form

An arc laps the disc, running leftward over the top. Its length is the speed of its own eased ramp — head and tail are one curve offset by four frames — so it grows out of a point, opens to about 100° across the top, and contracts back into that same point. The point is not the right limb: it is where the first S becomes visible, a row higher, so the arc dies exactly where and when the S appears. Then one S per row flows through, right to left, staggered. The S's ease the same way the arc does, slow at both limbs and quickest across the middle, which shapes the stroke as well as its travel — each enters short, opens to about 68% of its path, and closes as it leaves. Then the mark assembles row by row, same direction. It turns in rather than wiping: each row's edge travels like a meridian on a sphere, x = 300 + 300 cos(πt), so it is nearly still at each limb and quickest across the centre — measured 5× between limb and centre speed. Then it holds and turns out the same way. The S's run on the mark's own edges, so the fill lands on the line that predicted it. Real mark geometry, imported from meridian_black.svg.

Every movement carries a coloured wake. A ghost is not a copy that has been moved — it is the same path with the same trim, run three frames late, so the wake is the stroke's own past and nothing about the geometry or the schedule changes. Its length is the stroke's speed — and at a one-frame lag that makes the colour a flash rather than a wake: it opens only where a stroke is quickest and is gone at both limbs, so you catch it rather than watch it. The mark's rows work the other way round, because a row is revealed by a matte rather than drawn by a trim: each ghost is on screen slightly longer at both ends, so colour runs ahead of the ink as a row arrives and is left behind as it leaves. The hues are --m-seq-500/300 — the Meridian blue-240 sequential ramp from the palette gallery, read from tokens.css, not picked. Other schemes and lags are side by side on schemes.html.
