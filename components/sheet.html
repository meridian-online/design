Component sheet

The twelve components the design review adds to meridian-egui, each drawn in every state its entry lists, in light and dark, over the emitted tokens.css. Where a look was open, the candidates are lettered and kept beside the one that was ruled. Hover and pressed states are drawn fixed, so nothing here depends on where the pointer is. Each component ends with the pick, its reason and the ruling; this page with its rulings is the reference each component is built and reviewed against.

0 · What is already settled drawn for comparison, not reopened

The keycap, the list row and the line tabs, as key_chip, list_row and line_tabs draw them today. One measurement governs much of what follows: the keycap is 21 tall. It fits a 24 row, which draws no ring. It does not fit inside a 24 control, whose focus ring is 2px inside its edge and leaves 20. Section 1 settles what a key looks like inside a control.

1 · Button earned by 32 hand-built buttons in Brightfield; the ribbon command is this, laid out in a row

An optional icon, a label and an optional keycap, in three weights. The default weight is a sunken fill inside a rule. The primary weight is the accent fill, for the one action a surface is for. The quiet weight has no fill and no rule until the pointer is on it, and is what a toolbar and the ribbon use. An unavailable button keeps its place and its keycap, and reports its reason for the popover (5).

Pick: A for the key inside a control. It keeps the keycap's look, its foot included, and the ribbon keeps its 24 rung. It does mean the keycap has two sizes, 21 in a row and 16 in a control, which the chrome guideline's "it keeps the key chip's size" has to be rewritten to allow; the alternative that keeps one size is to make every keycap 16. B has no overlap by construction and loses the cap. C keeps everything and no longer fits the 32 band.

Pick: B and B for the two keycap inks. On the accent fill the grey chip reads as a hole in the button; the on-solid keycap keeps the chip's size and foot. Dimming an unavailable command's key with its label says the key will not act either, which is true, and the chip's box still shows where the key is.

Ruling, 22 November 2026: a key inside a control is A, the smaller cap. It is 16 tall and sits 4 from the control's top, bottom and trailing edge, so it clears the focus ring by 2 on all three. The two keycap inks are B and B: the on-solid ink on a primary button, and dimmed with its label on an unavailable command.

2 · Text field earned by 14 hand-built inputs; wraps egui's own text editing

The guideline already fixes most of this: a field is a sunken fill over a 1px rule, and while it has the keys the rule is 2px in the focus ink. What is open is whether the fill is also boxed, as the mockups drew it. A field carries an optional leading icon and an optional unit. It draws its own message line beneath, for help or an error; its label is laid out by the form that holds it.

Aa rule under the fill
Ba box with a heavier foot

Pick: A. It is what the chrome guideline says, and it keeps a field from reading as a button, which is the boxed sunken fill in (1).

Ruling, 22 November 2026: the pick stands.

3 · Ribbon tabs  and  4 · Ribbon row

The stage tabs, then the contextual tab set apart by a divider and a tint and named for the focused activity and node. The row beneath holds quiet buttons (1) in groups divided by a rule, in a 32 band with its contents at the 24 rung. The open tab must not read as a line tab, whose mark is a bar under the word.

Pick: A. The joined cell says which tab owns the row beneath, which a bar cannot, and it is the one treatment here that cannot be taken for a line tab. Collapsed, the cell goes and only the heavier word remains, so selected and open stay distinct. One thing to settle with the tint: the contextual tab uses the Maritime wash, and Maritime otherwise means interaction; the alternative is the neutral header fill with the divider alone.

Ruling, 22 November 2026: the pick stands.

5 · Popover the reason popover and the anchored layer as one component

An overlay card anchored below a rect, or above it when there is no room below, holding any body. It casts the overlay shadow (3px), not the modal's (6px), and has no arrow. The reason form is one sentence and, when given, the command's keycap. The width scale has only the modal's 480 and 560, which are too wide for a sentence, so a popover width is a new token.

Pick: 240 for the reason form, with the general form sized by its body. A reason is one sentence, and at 320 a short one leaves the card half empty.

Ruling, 22 November 2026: the pick stands.

6 · Segmented control the activity switcher

A small set of mutually exclusive options in a pane header, each with its keycap. It must not read as a tab strip. Because it carries keycaps it fills the header's 24; without them it draws at 20.

Aa raised cell on a track
Bthe selection wash in a ruled box

Pick: B. The chrome guideline keeps the selection wash for a toggle that is on, and an option here is one. The ruled box makes it one control with parts, where the track in A sits on the header's own grey and loses its edge.

Ruling, 22 November 2026: the pick stands.

7 · Section with a count  and  8 · Distribution bar

A section is a header row (a disclosure, a title, an optional count) and a body drawn only while it is open. The distribution bar draws a column's values in chart ink: equal bins low to high for a number, the top values for a category with the others as one bar, and the missing count in the null ink. The bar under the pointer is reported back, and Brightfield writes the line beneath.

Aa small capital label over a hairline, the count at the right
Bthe pane header's band, the count beside the title
Distribution barcandidates for a category's top values

Pick: A for the section, A for the category. The small capital label is the detail the mockups took from Foundry, and it leaves the pane header as the only grey band in a rail, so a stack of sections reads as one pane. It needs letter-spacing, which no token carries yet. For categories, bars keep one shape for both kinds of column, and a bar's height can be compared where a segment's width in a stack cannot.

Ruling, 22 November 2026: the pick stands.

9 · Menu item

A list row with a check column, a label, a right-aligned keycap and, for a submenu, a chevron. It takes the list row's three states. Unavailable, it is greyed, keeps its keycap, and still takes the cursor so its reason can be read from the keys. The picker row's disabled state is the same treatment and is not a component of its own.

One look. It follows the list row and the unavailable ruling in (1).

Ruling, 22 November 2026: the pick stands.

10 · Step row the spine row, with its state mark and its thin form

One row per step or table: the graph's edges in a gutter at the leading edge, the state mark on its lane, the name, what it produced, and for a step a timeline bar and a duration. A folded group shows a fold control and its member count. The row has two marks of place. The cursor is the list row's: the row the keys act on. Focused is the node the canvas shows.

The state mark's shape and ink per state is plain data in meridian-design, so the graph Brightfield draws and this row read one definition. Drawing the mark on its lane, as a commit graph does, is my call; the chrome plan lists the gutter and the mark as separate columns.

Pick: timeline B. Measured from a live Flight Explainer run: its rows are 30, its bar is 16 with a 1px rule and a 2px corner, and its marks sit on their lane in the gutter, 7 across with a 1.5 stroke, as they are drawn here. At 6 the bar reads as an underline; at half the row it reads as a span of time. Flight Explainer draws its marks in one grey and tells state apart by fill alone; the marks here keep an ink per state as well.

Pick: marks B, focused A. Round marks told apart by ink alone fail for a reader who cannot tell red from green, and the icons at 14 are busier than a list of forty steps wants. The shapes are a ring, a half disc, a disc, a diamond and a ring with a centre: five outlines that differ in grey. For the focused step, the selection wash is what the guideline keeps for a thing that is selected, and it stays visible beside the cursor's bar when both land on one row.

Ruling, 22 November 2026: the pick stands.
